# Editorial Proof

Editorial Proof treats professional experience as evidence arranged with calm authority. Space is generous but purposeful; a strong title row establishes identity, and a measured column of statements turns complexity into a credible narrative. Every margin and baseline must feel meticulously adjusted by an expert editor.

Typography carries almost the entire composition. Weight, rhythm, indentation, and line length create hierarchy without decorative devices. The result should appear labored over with care, with technical density made readable through painstaking typesetting rather than simplification.

Color remains restrained: near-black for substance, deep navy for the project identity, and quiet gray for time and supporting context. A single fine rule provides structure. Every tonal choice must be calibrated with master-level precision so the page feels professional in both a résumé and a portfolio.

Scale follows proof. The project name leads, numbered contributions form the body, and quantified outcomes close the composition with greater weight. Text is concise, specific, and evidence-led. Craftsmanship is visible in wrapping, punctuation, hanging indents, and the consistent distance between ideas.

The final artifact should survive both rapid scanning and close reading. In seconds it communicates role, scope, and measurable result; on inspection it reveals architecture and engineering depth. It must look meticulously crafted, trustworthy, and ready for executive review.
